#KyivNotKiev Research

A Large-Scale Computational Study of Ukrainian Toponym Adoption Across 9 Media Platforms

Academic Research · Non-Commercial · University Project

What This Research Studies

In 2018, Ukraine launched the #KyivNotKiev campaign asking English-language media to adopt Ukrainian transliterations of place names instead of Russian-derived ones. Our study measures how and when this adoption happened across 9 independent data sources, 48 toponym pairs, and 330+ million text matches from 2010–2025.

Research question: How did English-language media adopt Ukrainian spellings (Kyiv, Kharkiv, Odesa) over Russian spellings (Kiev, Kharkov, Odessa) — and does the pattern differ by platform, topic, and time?

YouTube API Data Pipeline

Our pipeline uses two YouTube API endpoints to collect video metadata:

search.list→ Find videos by term + year→ videos.list→ Get metadata→ Classify variant→ Aggregate ratios
# Step 1: Search for each spelling variant per year youtube.search().list( q="Kyiv", publishedAfter="2022-01-01T00:00:00Z", publishedBefore="2022-12-31T23:59:59Z", type="video", maxResults=50 ) # Step 2: Batch fetch metadata (1 unit per 50 videos) youtube.videos().list( part="snippet,statistics", id="video_id_1,video_id_2,..." ) # Step 3: Classify variant from title title = "Walking tour of Kyiv, Ukraine 2024" variant = "ukrainian" # contains "Kyiv" not "Kiev"

What we store

Only video metadata: video_id title publishedAt viewCount channelTitle

No video content, thumbnails, comments, or user data is collected or stored.

Output: Adoption Timeseries

The end result is an aggregate adoption ratio per year — what percentage of YouTube videos use the Ukrainian vs Russian spelling. This is displayed alongside other sources on our research website.

Example: Kyiv adoption across sources

Each line represents a different data source. YouTube (red) shows the same adoption curve as News (dark blue) and Reddit (orange).

Key finding from YouTube data: In 2018, only 28% of YouTube videos about the Ukrainian capital used "Kyiv." By 2022 (after the full-scale invasion), adoption reached 72%. By 2024, it stabilized at 85%+ — consistent with the pattern observed across all 9 sources.

Cross-Platform Analysis

YouTube data is combined with 8 other sources to produce cross-platform adoption curves. We also apply unsupervised machine learning (sentence embeddings + HDBSCAN clustering) to discover how spelling variants create distinct discourse communities.

Timeseries Comparison

Side-by-side adoption curves from News, YouTube, Reddit, Academic papers, Books, and more. Shows whether platforms adopt at different rates.

Embedding Clustering

Texts are embedded using sentence-transformers, reduced via UMAP, and clustered with HDBSCAN. Reveals that spelling variants occupy different semantic spaces.

Statistical Contrast

Log-odds ratio analysis comparing vocabulary between Ukrainian and Russian spelling texts. Shows systematic differences in discourse context.

Verified Holdouts

For each pair, 50+ verified articles from tier-1 media (BBC, Reuters, Guardian) with extracted text, variant classification, and source URLs.
